# R08 How many markets and price updates each venue carries, when it peaks, and what the rate limits allow

Scope note: the brief has not chosen the two venues yet (R01 owns that). This file measures Kalshi and Polymarket,
the two venues v1 used (brief Parking lot P-09), as candidates only. The first-pass live figures were measured on
2026-10-11 between 19:39 and 19:55 UTC (14:39-14:55 CDT), a Sunday with NFL games in progress, from public endpoints
with no credentials. An adversarial verification pass re-measured some of them between 19:57 and 20:03 UTC; where
the two differ, this file says so.

## Answer

Measured live today, Kalshi lists about 126,000 open single (non-combo) markets (126,593 at 19:39 UTC and 126,164
at 19:58 UTC, in 127 pages of 1,000) and Polymarket lists 248,855 open markets in 2,489 pages of 100; only 17.8% and
7.9% of them traded in the last 24 hours, and the 10 busiest markets on each venue changed price in 0% to 12.4% of
the day's minutes (S04.8, S08.4, S08.7, S08.8). Kalshi publishes limits for authenticated requests only (Basic tier:
200 read tokens per second at 10 tokens per GET, about 20 requests per second, with a 3-second burst) and Polymarket
publishes per-IP throttles (such as 300 per 10 s on Gamma /markets and 1,500 per 10 s on CLOB /book), so one full
listing sweep needs at least about 6 s of Kalshi budget and between about 6 s and 83 s of Polymarket budget,
depending on which throttle line covers its keyset endpoint (S08.1, S08.2). Exact Kalshi trade counts in sampled
minutes ran from 3,342 (02:29 CDT Sunday) to 25,978 (21:29 CDT Saturday), about 7.8 times as many, and Kalshi pauses
trading every Thursday 3-5 AM ET (S08.5, S08.11). Confidence: medium, because Kalshi's limit for unauthenticated
reads and how Polymarket bills its keyset listing endpoint are both undocumented.

## Takeaway

The two venues list about 375,000 open markets between them but fewer than one in five traded in the last day, and
while Kalshi's documented budget covers a full sweep in seconds, Polymarket's sweep is about 20 times as many requests
under throttle lines its docs do not pin to that endpoint.

## Findings

- **F1** [LB] Kalshi rate limits are token buckets: "Every authenticated request costs tokens", most requests cost the default 10 tokens, and the Basic tier's Read budget is 200 tokens/s (Advanced 300, up to Prestige 12,000). Basic and Advanced Read buckets hold three seconds of budget, which allows a burst of up to 3× the per-second budget. A limited request gets HTTP 429 with no Retry-After or X-RateLimit headers and no penalty, and the page says to back off exponentially. Expert and higher tiers are earned from trading volume or assigned by Kalshi. The page states no budget for unauthenticated requests. — S08.1 (A), §Token-based limits, §Bucket capacity and bursting, §When you hit the limit, §Tiers and budgets, §Tier qualification
- **F2** [LB] Polymarket's limits are per IP and enforced by Cloudflare: over the limit, requests are throttled (delayed or queued) rather than immediately rejected, on sliding windows. General: 15,000/10 s. Gamma: general 4,000/10 s, `/events` 500/10 s, `/markets` 300/10 s, `/markets` + `/events` listing 900/10 s. CLOB market data: `/book` 1,500/10 s, `/books` 500/10 s, `/prices-history` 1,000/10 s. Data API v1 `/trades` 200/10 s. The table does not list the `/markets/keyset` path on its own line. — S08.2 (A), §General, §Gamma API, §CLOB API Market Data, §Data API

## Inferences

<!-- Reasoning across findings. Says what follows and how sure; never what to choose. -->

- **I1** from F1 + F3: a full Kalshi sweep of open non-combo markets fits inside a minute of Basic-tier budget. Formula: 127 pages ÷ (200 tokens/s ÷ 10 tokens per GET = 20 requests/s) = 6.4 s of budget per sweep, and 127 ÷ (20 × 60 = 1,200 requests per minute) ≈ 11% of one minute's budget. Assumptions: A-1 unauthenticated reads get a Basic-tier budget (undocumented; the verification pass drew one 429 in an unpaced 127-page crawl, so some unauthenticated limit exists, size unknown); A-2 1,000 markets per page; A-3 combo markets excluded. Range: low 6.4 s (requests issued at the full 20/s), base 13-32 s (127 × 0.10 s median request time; 32 s measured unpaced including parsing, one 429), high 50 s (measured, with 25 s of deliberate pauses). Not stated by any single source; medium confidence because A-1 is unverified.
- **I2** from F2 + F4 + F16: a full Polymarket sweep may or may not fit in a minute inside the published throttles, depending on which line covers `/markets/keyset`. Formula: 2,489 pages ÷ (300 requests per 10 s = 30 requests/s) = 83 s; ÷ (900 per 10 s listing line = 90/s) ≈ 28 s; ÷ (4,000 per 10 s Gamma general = 400/s) ≈ 6 s. Assumptions: A-1 the docs do not list the keyset path separately; A-2 100 markets per page; A-3 the docs say Cloudflare delays requests rather than rejecting them, but F16 shows a 403 rejection well below every listed line (cause unknown), so the published lines may not be the binding limit. Range: low about 6 s (general line), middle about 28 s (listing line), base 83 s (/markets line), high 751 s (measured, sequential). Low-to-medium confidence.
- **I3** from F1 + F2 + F3 + F4: refreshing prices for a matched set of N markets one book request at a time has a floor set by Kalshi. Formula: refresh period T = N ÷ R, with R = 20 requests/s for Kalshi (Basic, A-1 of I1) and 150 requests/s for Polymarket `/book` (1,500 per 10 s). Assumptions: one request per market per refresh, no batching (batch book endpoints are v1 leads, not re-read; see Gaps), and N set by R02's overlap count. Range: N = 100 gives Kalshi 5 s and Polymarket 0.7 s; N = 1,000 (base) gives 50 s and 6.7 s; N = 10,000 gives 500 s and 67 s. Medium confidence.
- **I4** from F5 + F10 + F12: Kalshi's load swings by about 7.8× within one weekend, with a quiet overnight window and a fixed weekly pause. Formula: peak factor = 25,978 ÷ 3,342 ≈ 7.8, peak ≈ 25,978 ÷ 60 ≈ 433 trades per second, trough ≈ 3,342 ÷ 60 ≈ 56 trades per second. Assumptions: one weekend (Sat-Sun 2026-10-10/11, college and NFL football) plus three weekday windows; exact 60 s counts from hh:29:30, one minute per hour. Range: weekdays 4,116-7,048 trades per minute (3 windows, outside the pause); the weekend 3,342-25,978, with Saturday 14:29-22:29 CDT at 11,811-25,978. The quietest sampled stretch is 02:29-06:29 CDT; Thursday 03:00-05:00 ET (02:00-04:00 CDT) is a scheduled trading pause. Low-to-medium confidence: a single weekend and single-minute samples.
- **I5** from F3 + F4 + F8 + F9: a cached price rarely goes stale minute to minute, even in the busiest markets. Formula: the chance that a 60 s poll sees a new price is about changed minutes ÷ 1,440. For Kalshi's top 10 that is 92/1,440 to 179/1,440 = 6.4%-12.4% (base about 163/1,440 ≈ 11%); for Polymarket's top 10 it is 0/1,440 to 113/1,440 = 0%-7.8% (base about 94/1,440 ≈ 6.5%). Across the whole universe, 82%-92% of markets did not trade in 24 h. Assumptions: last trade (Kalshi) or history price (Polymarket) at 1-minute resolution; changes in the order book below 1 minute were not measured; the sample is an NFL Sunday. Range: low 0%, base 6.5%-11%, high 12.4% of minutes. Medium confidence for the busiest markets; quote-level change rates are unknown.
- **I6** from F5 + F10 + F11: past spike sizes do not carry over as a multiplier, because the venue's baseline has grown. Formula: 2024 election-night minute (1,263) ÷ 2026 weekday 03:30 CDT minute (4,116) ≈ 0.31, and the 2026 weekend busy minutes (10,261-25,978, median 13,748) ÷ the weekday afternoon minute (7,048) ≈ 1.5-3.7. Assumptions: single 60 s windows; different market mixes and dates. Range: weekend-to-weekday ratio 1.5-3.7, base about 2. Low confidence: each point is a single minute.
- **I7** from F13 + F14: under a no-credentials constraint, Kalshi data is reachable only by REST polling, while Polymarket offers a public push stream. Formula: Kalshi gives 0 stream connections without a key; Polymarket gives 1 public market stream. Assumptions: the docs as read today; stream subscription limits are unread. Range: not numeric. High confidence on the access facts (both Tier A); the stream's capacity is unknown.
