# R07 Who already aggregates or routes across prediction markets, how big is the flow, and who is the customer?

## Answer

At least four products already pair equivalent listings across Kalshi and Polymarket: pmxt's hosted Router
(open-source SDK, 2.2k GitHub stars), River Markets (whose docs also split one order across member venues), Oddpool
(cross-venue data and arbitrage), and Dome until Polymarket bought it and set its APIs' end of life for 2026-04-28;
none of the sources read publishes a user count, routed volume or matching accuracy (S07.1, S05.4, S07.10, S07.11,
S07.12, S07.13, S07.8). The measurable flow sits with the venues and brokers: in the 24 hours to about 19:40Z on
2026-10-11, Kalshi's open markets traded 187.6M contracts and Polymarket's open events about $62M, and Robinhood's
hub traded 13.6B event contracts in Q2 2026 across 28.4M funded customers (S07.6, S07.5, S07.7); an inference from
that one Sunday puts the two venues at roughly $20B to $91B a year (base $49B), not a finding. Who the ideal customer
is remains an inference from vendor marketing (brokers and trading apps; trading firms and market makers), with
confidence medium overall because competitor usage is unpublished and the size rests on a single-day sample.

## Takeaway

Other companies already link "the same bet" across Kalshi and Polymarket and one splits orders between them, but
none says how often its links are wrong or how many people use it, while the big, measurable flow sits with the
venues and brokers like Robinhood.

## Findings

- **F1** [LB] pmxt describes itself as "CCXT for prediction markets", an MIT-licensed unified trading API listing 15 venues, including Polymarket, Polymarket US and Kalshi. Its hosted API trades Polymarket, Opinion and Limitless; Kalshi is self-hosted. The repository shows 2.2k stars and 275 forks (as of 2026-10-11). — S07.1 (A), repo header and README
- **F2** [LB] pmxt's hosted Router returns cross-venue market clusters. Each carries a relation type (identity, subset, superset, overlap or disjoint) and a 0-1 confidence score, and an API key is required. The page describes no order routing, no matching method and no accuracy figures. — S05.4 (A), Router › Matching
- **F3** [LB] River Markets' docs list Kalshi, Polymarket, Polymarket US and Crypto.com as available exchanges, Novig as coming soon and Rothera as a future integration. Each exchange listing gets its own River ID; a generic_asset_id groups equivalent listings into one cross-venue asset with an aggregated order book. A peg order sent on a generic asset places one leg per member venue, sized by where fills are expected, so one order is split across venues. The pages read do not say how equivalence is decided and give no user or volume figures. — S07.12 (A), Supported Exchanges; S07.10 (A), River IDs › One River ID per exchange listing; S07.11 (A), Order Types › Peg on a generic asset
- **F4** [LB] Oddpool's API docs describe data across Kalshi and Polymarket: per-venue historical order books and trades, WebSocket feeds, and cross-venue arbitrage and price-spread endpoints between the two venues. The docs navigation lists no order-placement endpoint. No user or volume figures are given. — S07.13 (A), docs home and navigation
- **F5** Oddpool's institutional sales page says a "symbology" layer maps the same outcome on both venues to one ID, paired with a verifier-confirmed match and a recorded confidence score. It names trading desks, market makers and research as users, shows Y Combinator backing, and states that only about a third of 250 matched events are settlement-fungible across both venues. These are the vendor's own marketing claims, not verified. — S07.4 (C), institutional page
- **F6** [LB] Dome was a data API across Polymarket and Kalshi that listed cross-platform market matching among its features; its docs navigation shows two matching-markets endpoints, both labelled sports, and an "Order Router" tab (not read). Its docs carry the banner "Dome has been acquired by Polymarket" and set end of life for all Dome APIs on 2026-04-28. No user or volume figures are given. — S07.8 (A), docs home banner and navigation
- **F7** [LB] Polymarket, live on 2026-10-11 from 19:41Z: 2,059 unique open events at offsets 0-2000, ordered by 24h volume and holding 52,009 nested markets. They summed to volume24hr $61.7M, volume1wk $174.8M and volume1mo $428.3M. Events tagged Sports, which may include esports, held $45.3M of the 24h figure. The last event in the set had $574 of 24h volume, and offsets above 2000 returned an error object instead of events (a verifier re-run at 19:51Z got 2,072 events, $61.8M 24h, $45.3M Sports, and HTTP 422 at offset 2100). — S07.5 (A), live API response
- **F8** [LB] Kalshi, live on 2026-10-11 from 19:42Z, all 127 pages with the cursor exhausted: 126,533 open markets excluding multivariate (parlay) markets, 22,680 of which traded in the last 24h. Their volume_24h_fp summed to 187.6M contracts, and volume_24h_fp × last_price_dollars came to about $72.2M (a verifier re-run at 19:52Z got 126,391 open markets, 194.9M contracts and $72.6M, the same order). NFL series led the top 12 by 24h contracts: KXNFLGAME 70.8M, KXNFLSPREAD 28.2M, KXNFLTOTAL 13.6M and KXNFLTD 13.3M. — S07.6 (A), live API response
- **F9** [LB] Robinhood's Q2 2026 release (2026-07-29) reports a "record 13.6 billion" event contracts traded through its Prediction Markets Hub, up over 10x, with $156M of event-contracts revenue and 28.4M funded customers. Rothera, launched in June 2026 as a CFTC-licensed exchange and clearinghouse through a joint venture with Susquehanna, has had over 3.5B contracts traded to date. The release names no other venue for the hub. — S07.7 (A), Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1
- **F10** Robinhood defines Event Contracts Traded as all event contracts bought or sold through the hub over a period; contracts trade in $0.01 steps up to $1 and settle at $1. — S07.7 (A), metric definition in the release
- **F11** The partner is PEAK6, whose core brands include Apex Fintech Solutions. PEAK6 Labs explores infrastructure for non-traditional assets, including prediction markets. This is the brief's paraphrase of the partner documents. — S06.10 (A), brief.md §Hiring partner
- **F12** River Markets' Insights article (2026-09-07) calls River "prime brokerage for prediction markets" and describes smart order routing that sends a generic-asset order across member venues or restricts it to selected exchanges. Its example users are trading firms and market makers, election desks, and other brokerages and trading applications; it names no customers. Vendor marketing, not verified. — S07.3 (C), Insights article and footer

## Inferences

- **I1** from F1 + F2 + F3 + F4 + F6: Grouping equivalent listings across Kalshi and Polymarket already exists as a product at three live vendors and existed at a fourth before a venue bought it. Splitting one order across those venues is documented by at least one (River's peg on a generic asset). None of the sources read publishes a matching error rate, a user count or a routed volume. So what exists today is the capability without published measurement, and a measured error rate would be the part not found elsewhere in what was read. Medium confidence: other named products (see Gaps) were not read.
- **I2** from F7 + F8: annual traded volume of the two venues together (market size, venue-volume basis). Formula: (Kalshi 24h premium ≈ $72.2M + Polymarket 24h volume $61.7M) × 365 = $134M × 365 ≈ $49B per year. Assumptions: A-1, 2026-10-11 (an NFL Sunday) is a typical day; A-2, Kalshi dollars = contracts × last price, a stand-in for the traded price; A-3, Polymarket volume24hr is USDC traded (its definition was not read); A-4, open markets only, so markets settled in the last 24h, Kalshi parlays, Polymarket events beyond offset 2000 and Polymarket US are excluded, which biases every figure low; 2026 US dollars. Range: low $20B (A-1 changed: an average day = 0.405 × this Sunday, the ratio of Polymarket's 7-day average to its 24h figure on the same open events); high $91B (A-2 changed: Kalshi counted at $1 face per contract, 187.6M + 61.7M = $249M per day); base $49B. Low confidence in the base; a multi-week daily series would narrow the range.
- **I3** from F7 + F8 + F9: one broker front-end carries flow of the same order as a venue. Formula: 13.6B contracts ÷ 91 days (Q2 2026) ≈ 150M contracts a day through Robinhood's hub, against 187.6M contracts on Kalshi's open markets on 2026-10-11. Assumptions: a Robinhood customer buy or sell counts once; a Kalshi contract is counted once per matched trade; the two are different quarters and days. Range: a Q2 average only (no monthly breakdown was read); the comparison holds to within an order of magnitude, not as a ratio. Medium confidence.
- **I4** from F1 + F5 + F12 + F9 + F11: the users that cross-venue products describe in their marketing are trading firms, market makers and desks (River, Oddpool), developers through SDKs (pmxt), and brokerages and trading apps (River). Separately, a broker with 28.4M funded customers routes retail event-contract flow and has stood up its own exchange. The customer types that have order flow able to reach more than one venue are therefore (a) brokers and trading apps and (b) trading firms and market makers. Data and research buyers want the mapping without the routing. The partner's core brands include Apex Fintech Solutions (F11); whether that business holds type-(a) flow is R06's question, not settled here. Low confidence: the user descriptions are vendor marketing (Tier C), and no customer interview, survey or partner statement of target customer was read.
- **I5** from F7 + F8: on 2026-10-11, sports drove most of the 24h flow on both venues. Formula: Polymarket Sports-tagged $45.3M ÷ $61.7M = 0.73; Kalshi NFL series in the top 12 = 138.6M ÷ 187.6M contracts = 0.74 (a lower bound, since NFL series below the top 12 are not counted). Assumptions: one NFL Sunday; tags and series prefixes stand in for topic. Range: 0.73-0.74 today; weekday and off-season values are unmeasured. So most of the volume that could be routed across venues is likely in templated sports markets, if both venues list the same games (overlap is R02's question). Medium confidence for today, low for any other day.
- **I6** from I2: the slice of I2 that cross-venue routing could address (the serviceable market) depends on how much volume sits in listings found on both venues. Formula: SAM = $49B × s, where s = the share of volume in markets listed on both venues. Assumptions: s is unmeasured here (R02 measures overlap); all I2 assumptions carry over. Range: not computable until s is measured; at the base it is $4.9B per 10 points of s. No confidence in a number yet.
